*Sungjun Cho*

Main category: cs.CV

TL;DR: Rendering two views per optimizer step significantly improves 3D Gaussian Splatting performance in hybrid-capture scenarios (e.g., aerial + ground views), outperforming complex methods like gradient surgery or preconditioning — due to variance reduction, not structural pairing rules.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard 3DGS under-fits hybrid-capture datasets with large camera distance disparities (e.g., drone and ground views), achieving 1–3 dB lower PSNR; existing fixes (e.g., longer training, gradient corrections) are either computationally expensive or ineffective, motivating a search for the true structural lever behind performance gains.

Method: Systematically evaluate 6 compute-matched training modifications: doubling iterations (60K), GradNorm, direction-aware gradient surgery, projective preconditioning, confidence-gated sampling, and random two-view per step. Introduce a variance-decomposition framework to analyze gradient behavior under bimodal camera regimes, testing whether pairing strategy (near/far, random, active) affects PSNR beyond variance reduction.

Result: Only two-view-per-step improves PSNR consistently (+1–3 dB), regardless of pairing rule (near/far, random, or active). All other methods (including complex gradient manipulations) fall within seed variance. Variance decomposition shows between-regime gradient variance is small relative to within-regime variance — making pairing irrelevant; halving gradient variance via dual-view accumulation is the key. The effect transfers to Scaffold-GS and Pixel-GS.

Conclusion: The primary bottleneck in hybrid-capture 3DGS is not misaligned gradients or scale mismatch, but excessive gradient variance from single-view updates. Simply rendering two views per step reduces this variance, offering a simple, generalizable, and compute-efficient solution — redefining the understanding of what ‘works’ in this setting.

Abstract: Hybrid-capture novel view synthesis combines images at substantially different camera distances (e.g., aerial drone and ground-level views). Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), trained for 30K iterations with one rendered view per optimizer step, under-fits the minority regime by 1-3 dB on five hybrid-capture benchmarks. We isolate the lever that closes this gap.
  Among compute-matched alternatives -- vanilla 60K iterations, magnitude corrections (GradNorm), direction-aware near/far gradient surgery, projective preconditioning, confidence-gated sample-level surgery, and a random two-view-per-step control -- the simplest structural change wins: rendering two views per optimizer step. The pairing rule (geometry-defined near/far, random, or active loss-disparity) does not change PSNR beyond seed variance on any of the five scenes; the structural change of having two views per step does.

The two-view structural lever transfers cleanly to the Scaffold-GS and Pixel-GS backbones.
